Is therapy for you? You don't have to be in a crisis to benefit

20 July 2026 · By Sharon Tomlin

One of the biggest misconceptions about therapy is that you have to reach breaking point before asking for support. Many people believe they need to be struggling with anxiety, depression or a major life crisis before therapy is “justified”. The reality is very different — people come to therapy for many reasons, and often it's simply because something doesn't feel quite right.

Sometimes life is functioning well on the surface

You're going to work, looking after your family, keeping up with your responsibilities. From the outside everything appears fine, but internally something feels different. Perhaps you are carrying a persistent sense of worry, feeling emotionally drained, or noticing that life has become more about getting through each day than truly living it. You may not know exactly what's wrong — you simply know that something has changed. That is enough.

You might recognise yourself here

You might feel anxious or overwhelmed more often than you'd like, keep repeating the same patterns, or struggle with low self-esteem and self-criticism. You may feel disconnected from others or from yourself, find it difficult to set boundaries or say no, or be navigating grief, loss or significant life changes. Perhaps you want to understand how past experiences continue to affect you in the present, or you simply want space to think, reflect and grow. There doesn't have to be one defining event that brings you to therapy — sometimes it's the accumulation of life's experiences that quietly becomes too heavy to carry alone.

Understanding yourself differently

Many of the ways we think, feel and respond to the world develop long before we are aware of them. Our relationships, childhood experiences, losses, successes and disappointments all shape how we see ourselves and others. Sometimes these patterns continue to serve us; sometimes they begin to hold us back. You may notice yourself asking why you always put everyone else's needs first, why you find it difficult to trust people, why you react so strongly in certain situations, or why you feel anxious even when everything seems okay. Therapy isn't about judging those patterns — it's about becoming curious about them, and understanding where they come from often creates space for something different.

What therapy really offers

People sometimes imagine therapy is about being given advice or told how to fix their problems, but in reality counselling with me is much more collaborative than that. It offers a confidential, compassionate space where you can slow down and explore what's happening in your life without judgement or pressure. Together, we can make sense of your experiences, recognise patterns that may no longer be serving you, and discover ways of responding that feel more aligned with who you are today.

Taking the first step

Starting therapy can feel daunting, and it's natural to wonder whether you'll know what to say or whether your problems are “big enough”. The truth is there is no threshold you have to meet. Booking a free 30-minute consultation is simply an opportunity for us to meet, explore what has brought you here, and consider whether working together feels like the right fit. There is no obligation — just a conversation. Because sometimes the hardest part isn't therapy itself; it's giving yourself permission to begin.
